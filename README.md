# Trench Matchup Win Probability

Predicting NFL pass-block outcomes from player tracking data, and testing whether a model that reads the full trajectory of a rep beats one that only sees snapshots of it.

**[Dashboard walkthrough](dashboard/walkthrough/README.md)** · **[Technical writeup (PDF)](writeup/writeup.pdf)** · **[Slide deck (PDF)](slides/trench-matchup-deck.pdf)** · **[Limitations](limitations.md)**

## The question

Does a model that watches every frame of a blocker-rusher matchup predict its outcome better than a model given summary statistics of the same matchup at a few sampled instants? If so, pass-block outcomes depend on how a rep unfolds, not just how it starts and ends.

## Headline result

| Model | Input | AUC (shared test set) |
|---|---|---|
| XGBoost | Features sampled at the snap, ~1 second in, and the final frame | 0.757 |
| LSTM | Every tracked frame, snap to outcome | 0.813 |

LSTM AUC ranged from 0.806 to 0.821 across repeated training runs, so the gap holds beyond a single favorable run. The two models also disagree on how they use motion: XGBoost leans on a two-point closing-speed estimate, while the LSTM reads continuous speed directly and barely needs it.

## Highlights

**The model inferred positional difficulty on its own.** Position was never an explicit measure of matchup difficulty, yet the LSTM's expected win rate tracks the ordering of actual win rate by position almost exactly (centers highest, tackles lowest, a gap of about 5.5 points on both). As a result, win rate over expected (WROE) is nearly flat across positions. That is the metric doing its job: separating a real difficulty gradient from individual performance.

**Case study: Joel Bitonio.** Among 72 qualifying guards, Bitonio has the highest WROE (+4.7%), in a position where the average is slightly negative (-0.6%).

## What's in the dashboard

- **Blockers:** every qualifying blocker's win rate, expected win rate, and WROE, filterable and sortable
- **Teams:** rankings by division or league-wide
- **Matchup detail:** one blocker's season grouped by opponent and week
- **Play Viewer:** frame-by-frame replays with a live win-probability curve (requires data that is not included; see below)

## Reproducing the analysis

1. Download the data from the [NFL Big Data Bowl 2023 Kaggle competition](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/nfl-big-data-bowl-2023/data) and unzip it into a folder named `nfl-big-data-bowl-2023/` at the repo root.
2. Install Python 3.12 with pandas, numpy, pyarrow, scikit-learn, xgboost, shap, matplotlib, and PyTorch.
3. Run `notebooks/01` through `07` in order. See [`notebooks/README.md`](notebooks/README.md).
4. To view the dashboard locally, see [`dashboard/README.md`](dashboard/README.md).

Running notebook 07 creates the Play Viewer's data file, which is why that page does not work from a plain clone.

## Data and terms of use

This project uses the NFL Big Data Bowl 2023 competition data. Tracking data is from NFL Next Gen Stats, and blocking charts and pressure outcomes are from Pro Football Focus, both distributed through the competition. Under the competition rules the data is for non-commercial use, including academic research and education, and may not be redistributed, so **no competition data is included in this repository**. The files in `dashboard/data/` are aggregated summary statistics (win rates and snap counts by blocker, team, and matchup) derived from that data, not the data itself.

This is an independent, non-commercial portfolio project. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by the NFL, Next Gen Stats, or Pro Football Focus.

Team logos load from ESPN's image CDN, and player headshots load from URLs in the [nflverse players dataset](https://github.com/nflverse/nflverse-data). Neither is stored in this repository, and all remain the property of their owners.
